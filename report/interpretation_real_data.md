**Bottom line.** Between January 2015 and October 2026, rises in fertiliser prices went with higher wheat prices about five to six months later. The evidence is clearer for urea than for DAP, the effect is modest, and it is an association, not proof of cause.

* **Size.** Over the pre-set six-month window the cumulative association is 0.41 for urea (95% CI 0.17 to 0.65) and 0.40 for DAP (0.02 to 0.78). In words: a one-off 1% rise in the fertiliser price goes with wheat prices roughly 0.4% higher by the end of the window. The models explain little of wheat's month-to-month movement (adjusted R-squared 0.10 for urea, 0.04 for DAP).
* **Timing.** The association sits at months 5 and 6. Correlations peak there (urea 0.25 and 0.27; DAP 0.24 at month 6, and also 0.20 in the same month), and the exploratory 12-month model shows nothing beyond month 6. Months 1 to 3 show little. That is why the urea models restricted to months 0 to 3 are not significant, and why the rolling chart, which uses months 1 to 3 (a window set before the data were examined), hovers near zero. It does not contradict the main finding.
* **Urea is more convincing than DAP.** Urea stays significant when the sample ends in December 2025 (0.32, CI 0.08 to 0.57), when the March 2022 benchmark change is set to missing (0.29, CI 0.07 to 0.51), and in the 12-month model. Its six-lag Granger test is significant (p = 0.002). DAP is fragile: its interval only just excludes zero, it is not significant in the 0 to 3 or 0 to 12 month models with energy controls, and its Granger tests show nothing (smallest p = 0.19). Without energy controls DAP is significant at all three window lengths, which suggests part of its apparent link may run through energy prices.
* **Direction.** Wheat prices do not help predict later fertiliser prices at any lag tested (smallest p = 0.17), which fits fertiliser leading wheat rather than following it.
* **What I cannot say.** The 2021 to 2022 window has too few months to tell whether the relationship changed: every sub-period interval includes zero. I cannot rule out common shocks that moved both prices, such as the 2022 war in Ukraine or the general commodity cycle; only oil and gas are controlled for. I have not tested why the delay is about six months.
